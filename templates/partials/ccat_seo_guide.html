CCAT aptitude test preparation guide

Practise the main CCAT question types

The Criteria Cognitive Aptitude Test is a fast cognitive ability assessment. Preparation should combine familiarity with numerical, verbal, and non-verbal questions with quick decisions under a strict overall time limit.

Numerical and math questions

Practise number series, ratios, percentages, arithmetic, and short word problems. Estimation and elimination can often save time when answer options are far apart.

Verbal and logical reasoning

Work through analogies, vocabulary, sentence logic, and word relationships. Identify the exact rule before comparing the available answers.

Spatial and abstract reasoning

Learn to recognise rotations, transformations, visual sequences, and pattern rules. Track one changing feature at a time before combining multiple rules.

What does CCAT stand for?

CCAT stands for Criteria Cognitive Aptitude Test. It is a pre-employment cognitive assessment covering numerical, verbal, logical, and spatial skills.

How should I prepare for a CCAT assessment?

Learn the recurring question formats first, practise mental calculation and elimination, and then complete timed mixed drills. Avoid spending too long on one difficult item when the assessment rewards answering efficiently.
